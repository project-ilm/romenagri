# Romenagri: the missing Sanskrit characters, the underscore guards, and the nukta layer

Copyright (C) 1993-2026 Abhishek Choudhary. Free software under the GNU General Public License, version 2 or (at your option) any later version: see `COPYING`, `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`.

Twelve characters of the Devanagari block that Sanskrit and Vedic text needs had no place in the character chain of Hindawi (Unicode <-> ACII <-> Romenagri). **Patch 0001** adds them **without changing the architecture**: each of the three tables gets new rows at its end, the recursive-descent parser `rmn2acii.c` gets twelve new branches and one small helper, and nothing else in the converters changes.

**Patch 0002** applies, on your request of 25 October, the twelve guard rules of section 14.7 of the document to `rmn2acii.c`: eleven rules of the parser took the character behind their test without looking at it, and an underscore taken that way was lost. The guards are a separate patch on top of patch 0001, so that they can be taken off again.

**Patch 0003** applies, on your request of 4 November ("Just add a layer that decomposes the underlying nukta layer, and builds it back"), a layer to the two Unicode converters. Since your decisions of 8 November it has three parts that are kept apart. The **rule**: a letter with a nukta is a consonant and a nukta, however it arrives, so `uni2acii` writes each of the nine precomposed nukta letters that it had no row for (U+0958-U+095F and U+0934) as the byte of its consonant and the nukta byte `E9`, where it used to write the low byte of the code point. The **form**: what the two converters write for these letters, as one code point or as two, is yours to choose and is not a policy of the build: `asis` (the default, which is what they always wrote), `nfc` or `nfd`, by `-f`, by the environment variable `ROMENAGRI_NUKTA_FORM`, by `-DNUKTA_FORM=...` when compiling, or by `nukta_set_form()` in a program that includes `nukta.h`. The **warning**: `uni2acii -w` (or `ROMENAGRI_NUKTA_WARN=1`) reports each code point that falls back to its low byte, such as the é that collides with the nukta; it is off by default. It is one new header, `nukta.h`, and edits to `uni2acii.c`, `acii2uni.c` and the Makefile; no row of any table changes, and the decoder and the parser are not touched. I made it a **separate patch** on top of patch 0002 and not a part of patch 0001, so that it can be taken off again; if you want it folded into patch 0001, say so (section 15.9 of the document says what that involves). Your own example, the cluster ड़्व typed with the single code point U+095C, used to lose the ड़्, because the low byte of U+095C is a backslash, and the decoder drops it together with the halant behind it (the output was व alone); it now comes back as ड़्व, as ड and a nukta. Your cases C1 to C8 and the decision on each are in section 15.7 of the document, in Devanagari, and are summarised in "The nukta layer" below.

The package also contains the formal definition of how Romenagri transliteration works, written from the code and checked against it, with the rules for extending the chain to larger character sets, for settling conflicts between standards, and for missing sounds: **`Romenagri/TRANSLITERATION.md`** (start with sections 5.3, 7, 8 and 10); and **`CONVERSATION-NARRATIVE.md`**, the pointwise account of the whole conversation from 14 October on that you asked for on 25 October.

Three later questions are answered in the document: the fifteen keys that the parser asks for and the table lacks (section 11.3), which are **still not fixed**, as you said on 20 October; three formal questions about the underscore (section 14), whose repair is patch 0002; and what a layer for the nukta letters has to take care of (section 15), which is patch 0003. The summary is in "Later questions" below.

**The site (`romenagri-site/`).** On 9 November you sent a site that you had Grok build from the v4 zip (five pages and a notebook) and asked what could be used. What could be used is its plan (a front page with a demonstration, an explainer, the characters, the challenges, the documents), its look, the idea of a notebook, and the author's note on the danda that it carries. What could not be used is its tables, its demonstration and its notebook: they were typed by hand, 16 rows of its Unicode table and 29 of its key table differ from the headers (U+0905 is `A4` in `unicode.h`, the site has `A1`), two of the eleven nukta letters are missing from it, and it changed no converter code (the zip holds the pages, a notebook and a short README, nothing else). So the site in this package is built again, by `romenagri-site/build_site.py`, from the headers of `Romenagri/` and from runs of the compiled programs. Its demonstration is a JavaScript port of the four converters that `--check` compares with the C programs and with the Python models of `tests/` on more than 400,000 cases, and with twelve deliberately broken copies of it to show that the comparison notices. `romenagri-site/docs/` is ready for GitHub Pages; nothing was published. See `romenagri-site/README.md`; what is yours to confirm is the note on the danda (decision 10 below).

## The twelve characters

| Character | Unicode | ACII byte | Romenagri | saral (`acii2hin`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ऌ vocalic L | U+090C | `81` | `_li` | `lri` |
| ॡ vocalic LL | U+0961 | `82` | `_lee` | `lri` |
| ॠ vocalic RR | U+0960 | `83` | `_ree` | `ri` |
| ◌ॢ sign vocalic L | U+0962 | `84` | `^_li` | `^lri` |
| ◌ॣ sign vocalic LL | U+0963 | `85` | `^_lee` | `^lri` |
| ◌ॄ sign vocalic RR | U+0944 | `86` | `^_ree` | `^ri` |
| ॥ double danda | U+0965 | `87` | `_vd` | `\|\|` |
| ◌॑ udatta | U+0951 | `88` | `_vua` | (nothing) |
| ◌॒ anudatta | U+0952 | `89` | `_vna` | (nothing) |
| ◌॓ grave accent | U+0953 | `8A` | `_vga` | (nothing) |
| ◌॔ acute accent | U+0954 | `8B` | `_vca` | (nothing) |
| ॐ OM | U+0950 | `8C` | `_om` | `om` |

The bytes are the next free ones of the C1 block (`80`-`9F`); no byte that ISCII or the existing tables use was touched. The strings follow the rules of the parser: a family of letters shares `_`, a sign that attaches ends the way the nukta does, and the lint (`tools/check_tables.py`) checks that the parser reads every new key as one token and does not disturb its neighbours. Section 6 of the document states the rules; section 7 explains each choice.

## Later questions (answered in the document)

### The fifteen reserved keys (section 11.3; `tools/reserved_keys.py`)

The parser can ask for 102 keys, and 15 have no row in `acii.h`. When typed text reaches one of them, `lookup()` returns `NULL` and `rmn2acii` crashes (exit status 139) and writes nothing. The decoder never writes any of the fifteen, so the round trip is not affected. They are two different things:

- **Thirteen `...vra` keys**: `bvra dvra gvra jvra kvra pvra svra tvra chvra _dvra _rdvra _rrvra _tvra`. This is the Latin letter **`v` typed after a consonant**. Romenagri writes व as `w` (`swar` works), and the one `v` it accepts is the one of ळ: `lvr` selects the key `lvra`, which exists. The code that reads it is shared by all fourteen consonant stems, so every other stem followed by `v` (`svar`, `tvam`, `kvaath`, `jvaala`) asks for a key that no script has a letter for.
- **Two aspirates, `lha` and `_rrha`**: typed `lh` and `_rrh`, which is what a person types for the conjuncts ल्ह (दुल्हन, चूल्हा) and ऱ्ह (Marathi). Devanagari has no aspirated l or aspirated ऱ; Romenagri's own spelling of those conjuncts is `l_ha` and `_rr_ha`, because `h` after a stem means "aspirate" and ह is `_ha`.

Four ways to close them are compared in the table of section 11.3: **A** fall back to plain text, as `nv` and `nh` already do; **B** read the `h` as `_ha`, which gives ल्ह and ऱ्ह; **C** read `v` as `w`; **D** add rows. **None is applied.** `python3 tools/reserved_keys.py Romenagri` lists the fifteen with the typed form of each and what the C parser does with it, and compares everything else with the grammar of section 5.3, so that a fix can be checked while it is made (`--strict` exits with 1 until all fifteen are closed). With patch 0002 the strings `kv` and `kv_` (and the same after the other stems) no longer ask for `kvra`, because the guard falls back to the stem and `a`; `kvr`, `kva`, `lh` and `_rrh` still crash.

### The underscore: runs, lower case, joiners (section 14; `tests/test_underscore.py`; lint T8)

- **Runs of N underscores.** The parser reads a run from the left: every pair is one literal underscore (byte `5F`) and an odd last one is the marker of the letter that follows (a halant at the end of the text). That is a machine with two states, the parity of the run. Two states are necessary (`x` and `x_` followed by `ta` give different bytes) and sufficient; the language is regular, so no context-free power and no second pass are needed, and the only lookahead is the character after the run. For well-formed ACII the decoder writes every literal underscore doubled and a marker only directly in front of a letter (the lint holds the table to this, T8), so the two translators are inverse to each other for runs of any length: tested on 30,000 random texts with literal underscores in runs of up to 21 (5,000 in the quick run). Malformed ACII, such as a halant after a vowel (`A4 E8`), can make the decoder leave a lone underscore; the round-trip contract excludes it (section 14.3).
- **The defect, and patch 0002.** These laws held except at **eleven rules of `rmn2acii.c` that took the character behind their guard without looking at it** (six unchecked skips and five wildcards; twelve positions). On those, an escaped underscore was lost: Hindawi doubles every underscore before it calls the parser, and `tc_flag` came out as bytes without any underscore; `tv_set` crashed (a missing row); and after a long word a short one could read leftovers of the long one. **Twelve guard rules** repair this (section 14.7): the grammar has the same five states, reads every underscore as the laws say, never reads past the end, and is a total function once the fifteen rows exist. They were checked exhaustively on all 551,881 strings of up to four letters over `a-z` and `_` and on all 1,118,481 strings of up to five letters over the letters that the rules test, and each guard is needed. Patch 0002 puts them into `rmn2acii.c`; the C parser agrees with the grammar on all 1,232,540 strings of the differential test, and its output is the same whatever follows the terminator, on 1,670,362 strings. **The price** is that a few invalid inputs change meaning (`c_` is now the letter `c` and a marker, where it was CHA with its `h` missing): of the 394,419 short sequences of the old repertoire the guards change the output of 2,482, all ill-formed, and no sequence that was exact is broken and none that was broken is repaired; they change none of the 331,621 well-formed sequences of up to three bytes.
- **Lower-case input.** Nothing in the converters guarantees it: only two language styles of Hindawi's `std2hin.awk` run `tr 'A-Z' 'a-z'`. Capital letters are inert in the parser, and the fold keeps every run of underscores, but it is not harmless: `C__f` keeps its escaped underscore and the folded `c__f` lost it in the parser of patch 0001 alone (`B8 E8 66`; with the guards `63 5F 66`). Crash-resilience against arbitrary changes of the text follows by induction for the grammar with the guards and a complete table; it is **true for the C code except for three reasons that have nothing to do with the underscore**: the fifteen missing rows, the fixed buffers (a run of 1,025 letters; 10,240 decoded characters) and an empty stack in the decoder on a stray halant or nukta (for example `31 E9`). On 59,091 random mutations of valid spellings (truncate, change case, insert, delete, replace, escape, concatenate) the parser without the guards reads past the end on 1,455 and reaches a missing row on 160; the grammar with the guards reads past the end on none and reaches a missing row on 147, and the C parser crashes exactly on a mutation that reaches a missing row (48 of the 20,000 that were run on it, against 57 without the guards).
- **Joiners and operators.** The script's alphabet at the parser and the operators of a host language share exactly one character, the underscore, and telling its roles apart takes two states; everything else that separates script from host language is decided by the lexers, without memory. ZWJ and ZWNJ, spelled `_zwj` and `_zwn`, would cost two rules and two rows and no state, if they keep to the marker rule (and a free byte; section 10.3). Not added.

These are arguments by induction and exhaustive checks in finite ranges, held by `tests/test_underscore.py` (U1 to U9; `--full` widens the ranges, `--deep` goes to six letters). They are not machine-checked proofs; section 14.10 lists what is proved, what is tested and what is open.

### The nukta layer (section 15; `tests/test_nukta.py`; patch 0003)

Unicode has eleven letters that are a consonant and a nukta in one code point. `unicode.h` had a row for two of them (ऩ U+0929 and ऱ U+0931). For the other nine, the eight क़ ख़ ग़ ज़ ड़ ढ़ फ़ य़ (U+0958-U+095F) and ऴ (U+0934), `uni2acii` wrote the low byte of the code point: U+095C became a backslash, U+0934 the digit `4`. The layer repairs this in the two converters, without a new row and without a new byte, in three parts that are kept apart (your decisions of 8 November):

- **The rule (always):** a letter with a nukta is a consonant and a nukta. `uni2acii` writes a code point that `unicode.h` has no row for and `nukta.h` lists as the byte of its consonant and `E9`. This is how the sound of the modified consonant is there when the parser maps the consonants (your "level 1") and before it binds a halant, an adjunct consonant or a vowel sign (level 2). The table still comes first: U+0929 stays `C7`, U+0931 stays `D0`.
- **The form (the Unicode layer, your choice):** `asis`, the default, is exactly what the converters always wrote. With `nfc`, `acii2uni` writes `C6 E9`, `CF E9` and `D2 E9` as U+0929, U+0931 and U+0934, and `uni2acii` writes U+0928 U+093C and U+0930 U+093C as `C7` and `D0`; the eight letters that NFC leaves decomposed stay pairs. With `nfd`, all eleven are a consonant and a nukta in both programs. The form is chosen by `-f FORM` or `--form=FORM`, by `ROMENAGRI_NUKTA_FORM`, by `-DNUKTA_FORM=NUKTA_NFC` when compiling (`make unicode CFLAGS="-Wall -Wno-unused -DNUKTA_FORM=NUKTA_NFC"`), or by `nukta_set_form("nfc")` in a program that includes `nukta.h`; the flag wins over the environment, and that over the macro. Use the same form on both converters.
- **The warning (off by default):** `uni2acii -w`, or `ROMENAGRI_NUKTA_WARN=1`, reports on stderr each code point that has no ACII byte and is written as its low byte, once, with a note that this is a known bug of converters of this kind (é is written `E9`, the nukta byte). The bytes written are the same with and without it.
- **What changes for existing users:** the nine code points no longer turn into their low byte. Nothing else changes in the default form: `acii2uni` is the old converter byte for byte (on random streams and on all 64,516 pairs and both kinds of triples), `uni2acii` differs from the converter of patch 0001 in the nine letters only, and the other 65,525 code units of the BMP are written as before.
- **Since zip no. 4:** the default no longer rebuilds `C6 E9`, `CF E9` and `D2 E9` as single code points (that was `NUKTA_REBUILD=1`; it is now `-f nfc`), and `NUKTA_REBUILD`, with its option to rebuild all eleven, is gone (section 15.6).

Your eight cases and the decision on each, as `tests/test_nukta.py` prints them (section 15.7 of the document has the whole tables, with the code points and the bytes):

| | Case | Decision of 8 November, and the result |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | ड़ as U+095C, or ड and a nukta | both are `BF E9` (`_rda`), decomposed at level 1; the output is not fixed to NFC, the form is a separate layer; the eight letters come out as a consonant and a nukta in every form |
| C2 | ऩ ऱ ऴ against their pairs | uniform inside, a character and a nukta; no further decision. ऩ and ऱ have two ACII spellings, `C7` or `C6 E9`, `D0` or `CF E9` |
| C3 | न + halant + nukta | none taken: not rebuilt, not reordered; `E8 E9` is also ISCII's soft halant; `acii2rmn` still crashes on `C6 E8 E9` |
| C4 | नि़, न ़ | nothing to rebuild (NFC agrees); NFC or NFD is the user's option at every level, and the default is the old behaviour |
| C5 | नé | the é is a normal character that has no ACII byte, written `E9` as before; encodings are not mixed and not repaired; the warning layer reports it (off by default) |
| C6 | ड़्व with U+095C | no special case, resolved by C1: `BF E9 E8 D4`, Romenagri `_rdwa`, comes out as ड़्व in every form; before: `5C E8 D4`, `wa`, व |
| C7 | ऴ | `D2 E9`, Romenagri `lvrza`; nothing is assigned to `D3` |
| C8 | ऱ्ह, ल्ह and the like | code point to code point, no assumption about font shaping; the fifteen reserved keys are untouched (the typed `lh`, `_rrh` and `_rdvr` still crash `rmn2acii`) |

Two limits to know. The layer **does not reorder**: a nukta after a halant or an accent stays where it is, so the layer and Python's NFC or NFD agree only on text whose marks are in canonical order (193,147 of the 200,000 random streams of the test; on the other 6,853, 6,747 of them a nukta after a halant or an accent, NFC and NFD reorder and the layer does not). And the round trip is exact **only up to canonical equivalence**: U+095C goes in, U+0921 U+093C comes out, in every form.

## Using it

The patched directory is `Romenagri/`: `cd Romenagri && make all`. Or apply the changes to your own checkout:

```
cd <directory that holds Romenagri/>
patch -p1 < <this package>/patches/0001-add-missing-sanskrit-characters.patch
patch -p1 < <this package>/patches/0002-apply-underscore-guards.patch        # the guards: leave this one out if you do not want them
patch -p1 < <this package>/patches/0003-add-nukta-layer.patch                # the nukta layer: goes on top of 0001 and 0002
python3 -I <this package>/tools/apply_sanskrit.py <Romenagri directory>     # the five source edits of patch 0001, without the document
python3 -I <this package>/tools/apply_guards.py <Romenagri directory>       # the edit of patch 0002 to rmn2acii.c, without the document
python3 -I <this package>/tools/apply_nukta.py <Romenagri directory>        # the edits of patch 0003 and the new nukta.h, without the document
```

To take a patch off again, take them off last first: `patch -R -p1 < <this package>/patches/0003-add-nukta-layer.patch` for the nukta layer, then `patch -R -p1 < <this package>/patches/0002-apply-underscore-guards.patch` for the guards (the document goes back with each of them). Then reinstall Romenagri (`make install` there, as the repository's `install` script does before it builds Hindawi): the Hindawi tools run the installed filters.

## Checking it

Needs `gcc`, `make`, `flex` (with `-lfl`), `patch` and `python3`; nothing is installed, everything is built under `build/`.

```
tests/run_all.sh                           quick run: 21 steps, about two minutes
tests/run_all.sh --full --resume           the exhaustive run; every step leaves a marker, so an interrupted run goes on where it stopped
tests/run_all.sh --full --resume --only <steps>      only the named steps: `build`, or the names that the summary prints
                                                     (`underscore` stands for the nine steps underscore_U1 ... underscore_U9)
```

The script builds three trees side by side and compares them: `old` (this tree with all three patches taken off), `mid` (patch 0001 only) and `new` (all three). The exhaustive run is long, so it is made to be run in pieces. Every step writes `build/logs/<step>.log` and a marker `<step>.rc` (exit status, seconds, time of the end); `--resume` skips a step whose marker says that it passed, and the build too, as long as the files of `Romenagri/`, `patches/`, `tools/` and `tests/` and the settings are the same as when `build/logs/stamp` was written. The check of section 14 is nine steps, one for each of U1 to U9 (`python3 tests/test_underscore.py --only U1,U7` runs chosen ones). After `--only build` the steps do not depend on one another, so a pool of two workers can run them, the longest first, and a last `--full --resume` prints every step and the summary:

```
tests/run_all.sh --full --resume --only build
printf '%s\n' underscore_U7 underscore_U1 parser_vs_model roundtrip underscore_U5 underscore_U6 underscore_U8 underscore_U9 \
  underscore_U4 underscore_U2 underscore_U3 corpus limitations recipes unicode nukta saral table_lint_new reserved_keys \
  doc_tables_in_sync e2e_new | xargs -P 2 -I{} tests/run_all.sh --full --resume --only {}
tests/run_all.sh --full --resume
```

On the recorded run (two processors) this took 3 minutes 20 seconds in all, building included; the steps took 375 seconds together: `parser_vs_model` 126 s, `underscore_U7` 85 s, `roundtrip` 55 s, `underscore_U1` 37 s, `underscore_U5` 20 s, `nukta` 18 s, `underscore_U4` 10 s, `underscore_U8` 8 s, and three seconds or less for each of the others. (The check of section 14 was first one step, which in `--full` mode grew to 6 GB and was killed by the container's memory limit after ten minutes; as far as it was traced, the cause was the outputs of the unguarded parser, which writes up to 4,096 bytes for a string that it reads past the end, held in memory for all 1,670,362 strings. It now works in chunks, and measures the parser without the guards only on the 90,345 strings of the quick ranges, which is enough to show the difference: 11,000 of them fail it.)

Optional environment: `RMN_CORPUS` (more Hindi text files, separated by `:`), `HINDAWI_SRC` (the `Hindawi/` directory of the repository, to rebuild the shailis for the end-to-end test), `RMN_BASELINE`, `RMN_BUILD`, `RMN_SKIP_HANDWRITTEN`, `RMN_GRAMMAR`; see section 13 of the document. ## Decisions that are yours to confirm

1. **OM is `8C`, not `A0`.** ISCII's chart (and `iscii_map.csv`) put OM at `A0`. But `uni2acii` writes every character it has no row for as its low byte, and the no-break space U+00A0 has low byte `A0`: with OM there, each no-break space in a text would come back as ॐ. `A0` stays unused; test (e) of `tests/test_unicode.py` keeps everyday characters off the new bytes (section 10.3).
2. **The avagraha string is now `_s`.** Byte `EB` was written `_3`, which the lexer cannot deliver to the parser (digits never reach it), so the avagraha never survived `acii2rmn | rmn2acii`; and `rmn2acii` crashed on `_s`, a key its own code asks for. The byte did not change, so existing ACII files are unaffected (section 7.1).
3. **Real ISCII needs a rewrite, which is not included.** ICU and Apple write OM, vocalic L, LL, RR, their signs and the avagraha as two bytes (a code and the nukta), and the double danda as `EA EA` or `90`; ACII uses one byte each. The translation table is in section 10.5; the converters were not changed to do it.
4. **Not added, on purpose:** a byte of its own for U+0934 (a recipe is tested and described, section 10.4; it also changes what Hindawi does with Tamil and Malayalam; the nukta layer already carries U+0934 through the chain as `D2 E9`); U+200C ZERO WIDTH NON-JOINER and U+200D ZERO WIDTH JOINER, with ISCII's explicit and soft halant (`E8 E8`, `E8 E9`), which are format characters, not letters; the 24 other uncovered code points of the Devanagari block; Vedic Extensions and Devanagari Extended, which do not fit the 42 free bytes (section 10.1).

## What was found on the way

Almost all of this was already so before the patches. Details and measurements are in sections 11 and 14 of the document, and `tests/test_limitations.py` and `tests/test_underscore.py` hold each one in place.

- The parser can ask for 102 keys, and 15 have no row in `acii.h`: thirteen of the form stem + `vra` (`kvra`, `tvra`, `_dvra` and ten more: the letter `v` typed after a consonant) and the two aspirates `lha` and `_rrha` (typed `lh`, `_rrh`). The filter crashes on them, and typed text can reach them (section 11.3). **Not changed.**
- Eleven rules of the parser took the character after their guard without looking at it: six jumped over it, five kept it as plain text whatever it was. An underscore taken this way was lost, and at the end of the input the rule stepped over the terminator (section 14.6; `tc_flag` through Hindawi's escaping lost its underscore). **Repaired by patch 0002.**
- `rmn2acii` overflows its buffer at 1,025 letters in a row, and `acii2rmn` its stack at 10,240 decoded characters. The parser's buffer is never cleared, so after a long word a short one could read letters left over from it (with the guards it no longer does). **The overflows are not changed.**
- The danda is lost: `।` becomes the ASCII bar.
- `uni2acii` silently corrupts every character it has no row for: é comes back as a nukta, the soft hyphen as ऐ, the rupee sign as छ, ZWJ and ZWNJ as carriage return and form feed. The double danda used to come back as `e`; with patch 0001 it and the eleven other new characters come back correctly. **Not changed** for the characters outside Devanagari, but `uni2acii -w` (patch 0003, off by default) now reports each of them once; for the nine precomposed nukta letters see the next item.
- `uni2acii` wrote the low byte for the nine precomposed nukta letters (U+0958-U+095F, U+0934): ड़ became a backslash, and the decoder dropped it together with the halant behind it, so the cluster ड़्व typed with the single code point came back as व. **Repaired by patch 0003** (section 15). `acii2uni` wrote a consonant and a nukta as two code points, which is not NFC for ऩ ऱ ऴ; whether that matters depends on what reads the text next, so the default stays as it was and `-f nfc` and `-f nfd` are there for those who need one form.
- `acii2rmn` crashes on a nukta after a halant or a danda (ISCII's soft halant and avagraha) and drops the consonant before an explicit halant; dental DA or DHA followed by a nukta is misread.
- In Hindawi (outside `Romenagri/`, nothing changed): its keyword replacement also works inside identifiers; the Python shaili does not work on the master branch; `hincc` needs GNU awk.
